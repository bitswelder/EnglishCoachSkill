# Portuguese → English pitfalls

The mistakes below come from Portuguese structure, not carelessness. Naming the
Portuguese cause is usually the fastest way to make the correction stick.

## 1. "Difficulties to" / infinitive after a noun
- ❌ "I have difficulties to write in English."
- ✅ "I have difficulty **writing** in English." / "I find it hard **to write** in English."
- PT: *tenho dificuldade **a/em** escrever*. English uses **-ing** after most nouns +
  preposition: *difficulty in doing*, *interest in doing*, *experience in doing*.
- Also note: *difficulty* is usually uncountable here — "difficulty", not "difficulties".

## 2. Missing / extra articles (a, an, the)
- ❌ "I am developer." → ✅ "I am **a** developer."
- ❌ "The life is hard." → ✅ "Life is hard."
- PT drops the article before a profession (*sou programador*) and adds one before
  abstract nouns (*a vida*). English does the opposite.

## 3. Present simple vs present continuous
- ❌ "I work on this bug since Monday." → ✅ "I **have been working** on this bug since Monday."
- PT uses the present for ongoing-since actions (*trabalho nisto desde segunda*).
  English needs the present perfect continuous with *since* / *for*.

## 4. Preposition defaults
| Portuguese | Wrong English | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| depende **de** | depends of | depends **on** |
| chegar **a** casa | arrive at home | arrive **home** / get home |
| casado **com** | married with | married **to** |
| interessado **em** | interested on | interested **in** |
| responsável **por** | responsible of | responsible **for** |
| discutir **sobre** | discuss about | discuss (no preposition) |
| pensar **em** | think on | think **about** / think **of** |

## 5. False friends
| Portuguese | Looks like | Actually means | Use instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| actualmente | actually | in fact, really | **currently**, at the moment |
| eventualmente | eventually | in the end, finally | **possibly**, if needed |
| pretender | pretend | fake, act | **intend**, plan to |
| realizar | realize | become aware | **carry out**, perform |
| assistir | assist | help | **attend**, watch |
| sensível | sensible | reasonable | **sensitive** |
| library / livraria | library | the book-lending place | bookshop = *livraria*, library = *biblioteca* |
| push / puxar | push | empurrar | *puxar* = **pull** |

## 6. Word order with adjectives and adverbs
- ❌ "a solution simple" → ✅ "a **simple** solution" (adjective before noun, always)
- ❌ "I speak well English" → ✅ "I speak English **well**."
- ❌ "I like very much this idea" → ✅ "I like this idea **very much**." / "I really like this idea."

## 7. "People is" and other plural agreement
- ❌ "The people is waiting." → ✅ "The people **are** waiting."
- *people*, *police*, *staff* take plural verbs. *Information*, *advice*, *feedback*,
  *software* are uncountable: never *informations*, *advices*, *feedbacks*.

## 8. Double negatives
- ❌ "I don't know nothing." → ✅ "I don't know **anything**." / "I know nothing."
- PT allows *não sei nada*; English allows only one negative per clause.

## 9. "Make" vs "do"
- *make* a decision, a mistake, a change, progress, an effort, money
- *do* a task, the work, research, a test, business, your best
- ❌ "make a question" → ✅ "**ask** a question" (PT *fazer uma pergunta*)

## 10. Questions without inversion / without auxiliary
- ❌ "You know where is the file?" → ✅ "Do you know where **the file is**?"
- In an embedded question the word order stays statement-like: *where the file is*,
  not *where is the file*.

## 11. Verb + -ing vs + to
- Need **-ing**: enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, suggest, recommend, mind, consider, practise.
  ❌ "I suggest to use a cache" → ✅ "I suggest **using** a cache."
- Need **to**: want, need, decide, try, hope, plan, manage, offer, promise.
- ❌ "I'm looking forward to **hear** from you" → ✅ "looking forward to **hearing** from you"
  (*to* is a preposition here, not an infinitive).

## 12. Politeness and register in work email
Direct translations of Portuguese formality sound either stiff or unintentionally blunt.
- ❌ "I come by this means to inform you..." → ✅ "I'm writing to let you know..."
- ❌ "Send me the file." (reads as an order) → ✅ "Could you send me the file?"
- ❌ "I wait for your answer." → ✅ "I look forward to your reply." / "Let me know, thanks."
- *Dear Sir* is very formal and dated in tech. Use *Hi <name>,* or *Hello <name>,*.

## 13. Pronunciation traps
- **-ed endings**: *worked* = WORKT, *asked* = ASKT, *needed* = NEE-did. No extra vowel.
- **h** is pronounced: *have*, *hotel*, *behind* — not silent as in PT *homem*.
- **th**: *think* (tongue between teeth), not "fink" or "tink".
- Final **-s** and consonant clusters: *tests*, *asked*, *strengths* — PT tends to add a
  vowel ("testes"). Keep the cluster tight.
- **r** at the start: *red*, *right* — English *r*, not the PT rolled/guttural *rr*.
- Words with no PT equivalent stress pattern: *de-VE-lo-per*, *COM-fort-a-ble*,
  *AL-go-rithm*, *DA-ta-base*, *pa-RA-me-ter*.
